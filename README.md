# cybersecurity-risk-register
This project demonstrates the development of a cybersecurity risk register for a fictional financial services organization. The objective was to identify, assess, prioritize, and recommend treatments for cybersecurity risks using a likelihood × impact methodology.
Apex Financial Services — Cybersecurity Risk Register

Project Overview

This project demonstrates the development of a cybersecurity risk register for Apex Financial Services, a fictional financial services organization.

The purpose of the project was to identify cybersecurity risks that could affect the organization’s systems, data, employees, and business operations. Each risk was evaluated based on likelihood and potential business impact, prioritized according to its risk score, and assigned a recommended risk treatment.

This project was created as a practical demonstration of entry-level Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) and cybersecurity analyst skills.

Project Objectives

The objectives of this project were to:

* Identify common cybersecurity risks within a corporate environment.
* Evaluate the likelihood and potential impact of each risk.
* Calculate inherent and residual risk.
* Prioritize risks based on severity.
* Recommend security controls to reduce identified risks.
* Assign risk owners and remediation target dates.
* Demonstrate a structured approach to cybersecurity risk management.

Risk Assessment Methodology

The risk register uses a simple Likelihood × Impact methodology.

Likelihood

Likelihood measures how probable it is that a risk event could occur.

* 1 — Rare
* 2 — Unlikely
* 3 — Possible
* 4 — Likely
* 5 — Almost Certain

Impact

Impact measures the potential effect of the risk on the organization.

* 1 — Negligible
* 2 — Minor
* 3 — Moderate
* 4 — Major
* 5 — Severe

Risk Calculation

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Risk scores are categorized as:

Score	Rating
1–4	Low
5–9	Medium
10–14	High
15–25	Critical

Risk Areas Assessed

The assessment evaluated risks across several cybersecurity areas, including:

* Identity and access management
* Phishing and social engineering
* Endpoint security
* Vulnerability and patch management
* Cloud security
* Data protection
* Third-party/vendor risk
* Security logging and monitoring
* Incident response
* Backup and recovery
* Insider threats

Key Findings

The assessment identified several risks requiring significant attention.

Phishing and credential theft were identified as major concerns because compromised credentials could provide attackers with unauthorized access to corporate systems and sensitive information.

Ransomware was also identified as a significant risk because a successful attack could disrupt business operations, compromise data, and create substantial recovery costs.

Other important areas included excessive user privileges, unpatched systems, third-party vendor security, cloud misconfiguration, and insufficient security logging.

Recommended Security Controls

Recommended controls included:

* Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
* Least-privilege access
* Privileged access management
* Endpoint detection and response (EDR)
* Vulnerability scanning
* Risk-based patch management
* Security awareness training
* Security information and event management (SIEM)
* Data loss prevention (DLP)
* Immutable and tested backups
* Third-party security assessments
* Incident response playbooks
* Regular access reviews

Risk Treatment

The primary treatment strategy used in this project was risk mitigation.

Mitigation involves implementing security controls that reduce the likelihood or potential impact of a risk.

Other treatment options considered were:

* Transfer — Shift some risk through insurance, contracts, or third-party arrangements.
* Accept — Formally acknowledge and retain a risk when the cost of additional controls outweighs the benefit.
* Avoid — Eliminate the activity creating the risk.

Lessons Learned

This project demonstrated that cybersecurity risk management is not simply about identifying technical vulnerabilities. A risk analyst must also consider the potential business impact, prioritize risks, identify appropriate controls, assign ownership, and determine how remaining risk should be managed.

The project also reinforced the importance of documenting cybersecurity decisions in a consistent and understandable format so that technical and business stakeholders can make informed decisions.
